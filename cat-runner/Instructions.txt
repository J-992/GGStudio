WORKING TITLE

Rooftop Rascal: Cat Escape

HIGH-LEVEL CONCEPT

Create a third-person physics-based rooftop obstacle game.

The player controls a mischievous orange cat that has stolen a large grilled fish from a restaurant. The restaurant owner and two guard dogs chase the cat across a series of city rooftops.

The cat must sprint, slide, jump, stumble, bounce, and recover while navigating narrow rooftops, wooden planks, awnings, pipes, air-conditioning units, moving signs, collapsing roof tiles, clotheslines, chimneys, water towers, and large gaps between buildings.

The gameplay must feel extremely close to the physics-driven browser game commonly listed as “Crazy Cart” or “Labubu Rides a Cart”:

- Third-person trailing camera.
- Momentum-heavy movement.
- Deliberately unstable traversal.
- Wobbly steering and physical tilting.
- Speed must be managed carefully.
- Ramps and slopes materially affect velocity.
- Hard turns at high speed can cause sliding, spinning, or falling.
- Landing badly can cause the player to tumble.
- Narrow elevated paths and fatal drops.
- Simple controls but difficult mastery.
- Very fast restarting.
- Short “one more attempt” gameplay loop.

Do not copy the reference game’s characters, models, textures, sounds, UI, name, code, or level layouts. Recreate only the general gameplay principles and physics feel using completely original content.

CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

1. The cat steals a fish from the restaurant.
2. The restaurant owner shouts and releases two dogs.
3. The cat begins running across the rooftops.
4. The player navigates a short but challenging obstacle course.
5. Moving too slowly allows the pursuers to catch up.
6. Falling from a rooftop or being caught ends the attempt.
7. Restart the level almost instantly.
8. Reaching the safe rooftop completes the level.
9. Collected fish tokens unlock cosmetic cat appearances.
10. The next rooftop course becomes available.

The game should prioritize movement mastery rather than combat.

PLAYER PHYSICS

Do not use a traditional position-based character controller.

The player must be physics driven.

Create an invisible rigid-body controller underneath the visual cat model. Movement should be produced using forces, impulses, velocity adjustment, ground-normal alignment, and torque rather than directly teleporting the player.

The physics should create the following feel:

- Strong forward momentum.
- Limited lateral grip.
- Noticeable but controllable sliding.
- Steering becomes less forgiving at high speed.
- The cat can oversteer and rotate too far.
- Slopes naturally accelerate or slow the player.
- Ramps preserve momentum.
- Collisions can knock the cat sideways.
- Poor landings cause a temporary tumble.
- The player can recover when landing on a valid rooftop.
- Falling into the street results in failure.
- The player should feel slightly unstable but not randomly uncontrollable.

Use a fixed physics timestep of 1/60 second.

Expose all important physics values in one configuration object:

- Body mass.
- Gravity.
- Forward acceleration.
- Reverse acceleration.
- Maximum ground speed.
- Maximum airborne speed.
- Steering strength.
- Steering responsiveness at low speed.
- Steering responsiveness at high speed.
- Lateral friction.
- Ground friction.
- Linear damping.
- Angular damping.
- Jump impulse.
- Air control.
- Landing stabilization.
- Maximum slope angle.
- Ground detection distance.
- Recovery delay.
- Collision knockback.
- Speed needed for camera FOV expansion.

Start with approximately these values, but tune them through playtesting:

- Gravity: approximately -18.
- Maximum running speed: approximately 14–17 world units per second.
- Jump impulse: approximately 6–8.
- Air control: approximately 20% of grounded steering.
- Low-speed steering should be responsive.
- High-speed steering should require smaller corrections.
- A full-speed hard turn should visibly slide the cat.
- A normal level should take approximately 45–90 seconds when completed cleanly.

Implement:

- Jump buffering.
- Approximately 100 ms of coyote time.
- Ground-normal detection.
- Slope acceleration.
- Reduced steering while airborne.
- Momentum-preserving jumps.
- Airborne rotation correction.
- Landing impact detection.
- Short stumble animation after hard landings.
- Automatic upright recovery if the cat tumbles on a safe surface.
- No recovery when the player has fallen off the level.

CHASE SYSTEM

The restaurant owner and dogs create constant pressure without requiring an expensive navigation system.

Implement a data-driven rooftop route using path nodes or a spline.

Calculate approximate player progress along this route.

The pursuers should follow the same route behind the player:

- Two dogs begin closer to the cat.
- The restaurant owner begins farther behind.
- The dogs move faster than the owner.
- Pursuers gain slightly when the player is slow.
- Pursuers lose distance when the player moves efficiently.
- They should jump or animate over gaps in a simplified scripted manner.
- They do not need full physics accuracy.
- They should remain visually believable.

Use chase rubber-banding carefully:

- The pursuers must remain visible or audible enough to maintain tension.
- They must not automatically catch a player who is moving well.
- Colliding with obstacles should allow them to gain distance.
- Standing still should result in being caught within several seconds.
- Being caught triggers a humorous animation rather than violence.

Display pursuit pressure using:

- Barking that grows louder as dogs approach.
- Footsteps and angry shouting from the owner.
- Edge-of-screen warning paw prints.
- Optional small distance indicator.
- No large intrusive chase meter during normal play.
